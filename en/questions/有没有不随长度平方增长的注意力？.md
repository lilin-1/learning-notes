# Is there attention that does not grow with the square of the length?

Yes, along two lines: look at fewer positions, or compress the history into a state of fixed size.

First, looking at fewer positions. [[sliding window attention|Sliding window attention]] looks only at the latest $w$ positions, so computation grows linearly with the length and distant information is relayed layer by layer. [[sparse attention|Sparse attention]] picks the $k$ most important positions by content, so key information far away can still be chosen. [[attention sink|Attention sinks]] show that when part of the cache is discarded, the first few tokens must stay.

Second, compressing into a state. [[linear attention|Linear attention]] replaces softmax with a feature map and accumulates the whole history into one matrix; a [[state space model]] summarizes it step by step in a hidden state regulated by the input. In generation, neither grows in computation or memory per step with the length, but both recall distant content less precisely, so they are often mixed with a few layers of full [[self-attention]].

Example: With a context of 100,000 tokens, full attention has each query look at 100,000 positions. A sliding window of 4096 looks at 4096, sparse attention selecting 2048 looks at 2048, and linear attention reads a single state of fixed size.
