# Why is the dot product in attention scaled?

To keep the inputs of [[softmax]] on a scale independent of the dimension, so that the weights do not collapse onto one position too early and the gradients do not vanish. If the components of a query and a key are independent with mean $0$ and variance $1$, the dot product $\sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$ has variance $d_k$; [[scaled dot-product attention]] divides by $\sqrt{d_k}$, bringing the variance of the scores back to $1$.

When the scores are too large, the output of softmax is nearly one-hot: the largest weight tends to $1$ and the rest to $0$. Since $\partial \alpha_i / \partial z_j = \alpha_i(\delta_{ij} - \alpha_j)$, all the derivatives then tend to $0$, the gradients that [[backpropagation]] passes back to the queries and keys vanish, and [[gradient descent]] can hardly update them.

Example: With $d_k = 64$, the dot product has standard deviation $8$. Scores that differ by $8$ give weights in the ratio $e^8 \approx 2981$; divided by $8$, the same gap corresponds to a ratio of only $e^1 \approx 2.7$.
