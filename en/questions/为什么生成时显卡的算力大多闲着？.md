# Why does most of a GPU's compute sit idle during generation?

Because the [[arithmetic intensity]] of each step is too low when tokens are generated one at a time. Each [[prefill and decode|decode]] step processes only one new token per sequence, so each weight read from GPU memory takes part in a single multiply–add. Yet the ratio of compute to bandwidth of a [[graphics processing unit|GPU]] is in the hundreds of operations per byte, so the arithmetic units spend most of their time waiting for data.

The remedy is to do more work per read, or simply to read less. [[continuous batching|Continuous batching]] lets many sequences share one read of the weights, [[speculative decoding]] verifies several tokens at once, and [[quantization]] directly reduces the bytes to be read. The [[KV cache]] differs from sequence to sequence and its reads cannot be shared, so the larger the batch, the greater its share of the reading.

Example: The critical intensity of the A100 is about 156 operations per byte. At batch size 1 the intensity is about 1, so at most $1/156$ of the compute is used, less than one percent; at batch size 64 the intensity is about 64, and still only about 40% is used.
