# Why are GPUs well suited to running neural networks?

Because the computation of a [[neural network]] is almost entirely matrix multiplication, whose many multiply–adds are independent of one another and can proceed simultaneously. The [[graphics processing unit|GPU]] is designed for exactly this: most of its chip area goes to arithmetic units, and a single [[kernel]] launch starts thousands of threads, each computing one piece of data.

In hardware, threads are grouped 32 at a time, and the threads of a [[warp]] execute the same instruction together, saving the cost of fetching instructions for each thread separately. Thread blocks are distributed over more than a hundred [[streaming multiprocessor|streaming multiprocessors]]. Each SM hosts many warps and switches to another whenever one waits for data; with high enough [[occupancy]], memory latency is hidden. Matrix multiplication goes to the [[Tensor Core|Tensor Cores]], which complete a small matrix multiplication in one instruction.

Example: Multiplying two $4096 \times 4096$ matrices takes $2 \times 4096^3 \approx 1.37 \times 10^{11}$ operations. At the 312 TFLOPS of the A100's Tensor Cores, that takes about 0.44 milliseconds.
