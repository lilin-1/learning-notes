# Where do the drafts for speculative decoding come from?

A draft must be both fast and accurate, and there are three sources. The most direct is a separate small model: from the same family as the large model, with the same vocabulary, and trained by [[knowledge distillation]] to imitate the large model, so that more of its guesses are right. The drawback is one more model to deploy.

The second source is small add-ons on the large model itself. [[Medusa]] adds a few decoding heads on top of the last layer, each guessing the 2nd, 3rd and later tokens, combined into a candidate tree verified at once. [[EAGLE]] uses a one-layer network that extrapolates at the feature level and guesses more accurately.

The third is prepared during training: [[multi-token prediction]] teaches the model to predict several following tokens at once, and at inference the extra modules serve directly as drafts. Whatever the source, the large model verifies the drafts in [[speculative decoding]], and the output distribution is the same as when the large model generates alone. Only when Medusa is fine-tuned together with the trunk does the trunk itself change.

Example: DeepSeek-V3's multi-token prediction module guesses the next token, which is accepted 85% to 90% of the time, and generation runs 1.8 times as fast.
