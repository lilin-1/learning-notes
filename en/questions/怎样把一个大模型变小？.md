# How can a large model be made smaller?

There are three routes: fewer bits per parameter, fewer parameters, or a new small model trained to imitate it.

[[quantization|Quantization]] is the most common: weights go from 16 bits to 8 or 4, reducing memory and the bytes read per step, and with methods such as [[GPTQ]] and [[AWQ]] the loss of accuracy is small. [[pruning|Pruning]] sets unimportant weights to zero, and regular formats such as 2:4 sparsity can be exploited directly by the [[Tensor Core|Tensor Cores]]. [[knowledge distillation|Knowledge distillation]] has a small model learn from the large model's outputs, yielding a genuinely smaller model at the cost of retraining.

The three can be combined: first distill a small model, then prune and quantize it.

Example: A 70-billion-parameter model in 16 bits takes about 140 GB. Quantized to 4 bits it takes about 35 GB; pruning half the weights to 2:4 leaves about 17.5 GB of nonzero values, plus a few bits to record where they are.
