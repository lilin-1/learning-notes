# How do thousands of GPUs train one model together?

By dividing parallelism into dimensions, each cutting one thing apart, and combining them. [[data parallelism|Data parallelism]] cuts the data: each group of GPUs processes different sequences of the batch, and the gradients are averaged at the end. [[tensor parallelism|Tensor parallelism]] cuts the matrices of each layer and communicates often, so it stays within one machine; [[pipeline parallelism]] cuts the layers into stages, communicates little and can span machines. [[expert parallelism|Expert parallelism]] spreads the experts of a [[mixture of experts]] over different GPUs, and [[context parallelism]] cuts long sequences into segments.

The product of the GPU counts of the dimensions is the total. Across the groups of data parallelism, [[ZeRO]] can further shard the optimizer state; when a group's batch is not large enough, [[gradient accumulation]] makes up the rest.

Example: Suppose there are 1024 GPUs, with tensor parallelism of 8, the GPUs of one machine, and pipeline parallelism of 8; data parallelism then has $1024 / (8 \times 8) = 16$ groups. Each group of 64 GPUs jointly holds one copy of the model, and each of the 16 groups processes $1/16$ of the batch.
