# How can a large model answer faster?

First be clear which speed is meant: the [[time to first token]] decides how soon the first word appears, and the [[inter-token latency]] how quickly each word after it follows. The two are governed by different factors and call for different remedies.

The time to first token is spent mainly in [[prefill and decode|prefill]]: [[prefix caching]] avoids recomputing shared prefixes, and [[tensor parallelism]] spreads the computation across several GPUs. The inter-token latency depends on each decode step: [[quantization]] reduces the weights read per step, [[speculative decoding]] verifies several tokens in one forward pass, and a [[CUDA graph]] removes the overhead of launching kernels one by one. [[chunked prefill|Chunked prefill]] prevents the stalls caused by long prompts cutting in.

Example: With an inter-token latency of 20 milliseconds, generating 500 tokens takes about 10 seconds. If speculative decoding yields 3.4 tokens per forward pass on average, this falls to about 3 seconds, plus the cost of the draft model.
