# How are reasoning models trained with reinforcement learning?

By letting the model attempt, again and again, problems whose answers can be checked automatically, rewarding it for correct answers, so that it learns on its own to write long, effective [[chain of thought|chains of thought]]. Math problems are checked by their final answers and programming problems by running tests; the reward comes from rules rather than a [[reward model]] and is therefore hard to exploit.

The algorithm is usually [[GRPO]]: it samples a group of responses for each problem and uses their relative quality within the group as the advantage, dispensing with a value model. DeepSeek-R1-Zero was trained this way directly on the pretrained model, without [[instruction tuning]] first; during training its responses grew longer and longer, and self-checking and self-correction appeared. The resulting model was hard to read and mixed languages, so DeepSeek-R1 first did a cold start on a small amount of demonstration data before reinforcement learning. The reasoning ability of a large model can also be passed to small models by [[knowledge distillation]].

Example: These figures are from the first version of the DeepSeek-R1 paper. R1-Zero's single-attempt accuracy on AIME 2024 rose from 15.6% to 71.0%, and to 86.7% with majority voting.
