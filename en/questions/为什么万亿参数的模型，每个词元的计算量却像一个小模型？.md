# Why does a trillion-parameter model compute like a small model for each token?

Because most such models are [[mixture of experts]] models: each [[token]] passes through only a few experts, and the [[active parameters]] involved are a small fraction of the total. Computation is determined by the active parameters, so a model with 1 trillion parameters, 32 billion of them active, computes about as much per token as a dense 32-billion-parameter model.

What is saved is computation, not memory. All the experts must still stay resident; 1 trillion parameters take about 1 TB even at 8 bits and can only be spread over many GPUs.

Example: DeepSeek-V3 has 671 billion parameters, about 37 billion of them, or 5.5%, active per token. The forward pass for each token takes about $2 \times 37$ billion, that is, $7.4 \times 10^{10}$ floating-point operations.
