# Why is inference on long texts slow and memory-hungry?

Because the computation of [[self-attention]] grows as $n^2$ with the length $n$, while the size of the [[KV cache]] grows linearly with $n$. For an input of length $n$, every layer computes $n \times n$ attention weights. In generation, every new [[token]] requires reading the cached keys and values of all $n$ preceding positions, and as the reads grow with $n$, each step slows down.

[[FlashAttention]] computes attention tile by tile without writing out the $n \times n$ weight matrix, saving that memory and traffic. [[grouped-query attention|Grouped-query attention]] lets several query heads share one set of keys and values, shrinking the cache to $g / h$ of its size. [[PagedAttention]] removes the waste of reserving the cache for the maximum length, and [[chunked prefill]] keeps one long prompt from blocking the other requests in its batch.

Example: For the smallest GPT-2, the KV cache for 1024 positions is about 38 MB. At a length of 32768 the cache grows 32-fold, to about 1.2 GB, and that is for a single sequence.
