# How can a model be made to answer as people prefer?

In two steps: first teach it to answer, then to answer well. [[instruction tuning|Instruction tuning]] trains the model on instructions with demonstration responses, so that it learns to respond to instructions. But “answering well” is hard to demonstrate word for word, while people easily judge which of two responses is better, so the second step uses comparison data instead.

One approach is [[reinforcement learning from human feedback|RLHF]]: a [[reward model]] trained on comparisons scores the responses, [[PPO]] raises the scores of the model's responses, and a [[KL divergence|KL penalty]] limits how far the model strays from the original. Another is [[DPO]]: using the identity between the optimal policy and the reward, it skips the reward model and reinforcement learning and trains directly on comparisons in a supervised way.

Example: After these three steps, people preferred the responses of a 1.3-billion-parameter InstructGPT model to those of GPT-3, with 100 times as many parameters.
