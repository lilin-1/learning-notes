A form of parallelism that places the experts of a [[mixture of experts]] layer on different GPUs. Once the router has chosen a token's experts, the token is sent to the GPUs holding them for computation and the results are sent back to its original GPU. Both transfers are all-to-all communication, in which every GPU sends data to every other.

Note: The experts hold most of the model's parameters. Spread out, they leave each GPU only a few experts to store, so memory drops sharply; and since each token passes through only a few experts, computation does not grow with their number. The price is two all-to-all transfers, especially slow across machines, so routing often limits each token to a few machines. With uneven load, the busiest GPU sets the time of the whole layer.

Example: 256 experts are spread over 32 GPUs, 8 per GPU. Each token selects 8 experts, so its representation goes to at most 8 GPUs, which send their results back after computing.

Paper: [GShard: Scaling Giant Models with Conditional Computation and Automatic Sharding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16668) (Lepikhin et al., 2020)
