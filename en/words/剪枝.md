Compressing a network by setting unimportant weights to zero. Unstructured pruning may remove weights at any position; structured pruning removes them in groups, such as whole rows or whole heads. Between the two, 2:4 sparsity requires at most 2 nonzero weights in every 4 adjacent ones; from Ampere on, [[Tensor Core|Tensor Cores]] can skip the zeros and double the throughput of matrix multiplication.

Note: Unstructured pruning preserves accuracy best but is hard to accelerate, since scattered zeros must still be read and computed on a GPU. Structured pruning makes the model directly smaller but loses more accuracy. 2:4 sparsity sits in between: the format is regular enough for hardware to exploit, and the loss of accuracy is small. SparseGPT, without retraining, pruned half the weights of a 175-billion-parameter model in one shot with almost no change in [[perplexity]].

Example: Pruning the weights $(0.9, -0.1, 0.05, -0.7)$ to 2:4 keeps the two with the largest absolute values, giving $(0.9, 0, 0, -0.7)$. The A100's 16-bit Tensor Cores reach 624 TFLOPS in this format, twice the dense rate.
