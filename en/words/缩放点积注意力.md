The [[attention mechanism]] whose scoring function is a scaled dot product, handling many queries at once in matrix form:

$$\mathrm{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V,$$

where the rows of $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$, $K \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_v}$ are the queries, keys and values, and [[softmax]] acts on each row. If the components of a query and a key are independent with mean $0$ and variance $1$, their dot product has variance $d_k$; dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ brings the variance back to $1$ and keeps softmax out of the saturated region where gradients are tiny.

Example: Let $d_k = 64$, and let a query's dot products with three keys be $(16, 8, 0)$. Unscaled, the weights are about $(0.9997, 0.0003, 0.0000)$, almost all in one place; divided by $8$, the scores become $(2, 1, 0)$ and the weights about $(0.67, 0.24, 0.09)$.
