A decoding method in which a small draft model guesses $k$ [[token|tokens]] in a row and the large model verifies them in parallel in one forward pass. The large model accepts or rejects the guesses from left to right, substitutes its own result at the first rejection and discards the guesses after it. With a suitable acceptance rule, the output distribution is exactly that of [[temperature sampling|sampling]] from the large model alone. Because [[prefill and decode|decoding]] is limited by memory bandwidth, verifying $k$ tokens at once takes about as long as generating 1, so the more guesses are right, the faster it goes.

Note: Whether it pays depends on the draft model being both fast and accurate. With a low acceptance rate, verification often fails within the first few positions, and the cost of drafting and verifying slows things down instead; with too large a draft model, the guesses themselves are expensive. It affects only speed, never the quality of the output.

Example: With $k = 4$, if each guess is accepted independently with probability $0.8$, a round yields on average $(1 - 0.8^5) / (1 - 0.8) \approx 3.4$ tokens, while the large model runs only 1 forward pass.

Paper: [Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192) (Leviathan et al., 2022)
Paper: [Accelerating Large Language Model Decoding with Speculative Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318) (Chen et al., 2023)
