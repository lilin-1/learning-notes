An iterative algorithm that repeatedly moves the parameters against the gradient of the [[loss function]]:

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta\, \nabla L(\theta_t),$$

where $\eta > 0$ is the learning rate. Estimating the gradient from a small batch of data at each step is called stochastic gradient descent.

Example: $L(\theta) = \theta^2$, $\theta_0 = 1$, $\eta = 0.1$. Since $\nabla L = 2\theta$, $\theta_1 = 0.8$ and $\theta_2 = 0.64$: each step multiplies $\theta$ by $0.8$, and the iterates converge to the minimum at $0$. With $\eta = 1.1$, each step multiplies $\theta$ by $-1.2$ and the iteration diverges.
