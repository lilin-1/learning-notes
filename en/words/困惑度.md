A measure of how well a [[language model]] predicts text: the exponential of the model's average per-[[token]] [[cross-entropy]] loss on a passage,

$$\mathrm{PPL} = \exp\Big(-\frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n} \ln P(x_t \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1})\Big),$$

where $n$ is the number of tokens in the passage and $P$ is the probability that the model, given the preceding text, assigns to the true next token. The lower the perplexity, the better the predictions; it is 1 when every token is predicted with probability 1.

Note: Perplexity is the reciprocal of the geometric mean of the tokens' probabilities. A perplexity of $k$ equals that of a model guessing uniformly among $k$ tokens at every step. Its logarithm is the average cross-entropy loss, so a loss of 2.0 corresponds to a perplexity of about 7.4. It depends on how text is split into tokens and on the test text; values from different vocabularies or test sets cannot be compared directly.

Example: A model gives "cat", "sat" and "down" the probabilities 0.1, 0.3 and 0.4 in turn. The average loss is $(-\ln 0.1 - \ln 0.3 - \ln 0.4)/3 \approx 1.474$ and the perplexity is $e^{1.474} \approx 4.37$, which also equals the product of the three, 0.012, raised to the power $-1/3$.
