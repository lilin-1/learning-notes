A compute unit of a [[graphics processing unit|GPU]], abbreviated SM. Each SM has its own arithmetic cores, registers and shared memory, and several schedulers that, every clock cycle, pick ready [[warp|warps]] from those resident and issue their next instruction. When a [[kernel]] is launched, its thread blocks are distributed across the SMs; a block runs on one SM from start to finish, and an SM can host several blocks at once when resources allow.

Note: With fewer blocks than SMs, some SMs have nothing to do; with slightly more blocks than a multiple of the SM count, only a few SMs work in the last round, which is called the tail effect. Kernels should therefore be split into many more blocks than there are SMs, or into an exact multiple of their number.

Example: The A100 has 108 SMs. Suppose each SM holds one block at a time and every block takes the same time. Then 216 blocks finish in exactly two rounds, while 217 need three, the third with only 1 SM working, so the total time grows by half.
