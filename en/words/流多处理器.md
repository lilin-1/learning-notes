A compute unit of a [[graphics processing unit|GPU]], abbreviated SM. On the A100, each SM is divided into 4 partitions. Each partition has one warp scheduler, a 64 KB register file, and 16 single-precision, 16 integer and 8 double-precision units plus one Tensor Core. The 4 partitions share 192 KB of combined L1 cache and shared memory. When a [[kernel]] is launched, its [[thread block|thread blocks]] are distributed across the SMs and the [[warp|warps]] of each block across the partitions; every cycle, a scheduler picks a ready warp and issues its next instruction.

Note: With fewer blocks than SMs, some SMs have nothing to do; with slightly more blocks than a multiple of the SM count, only a few SMs work in the last round, which is called the tail effect. Kernels should therefore be split into many more blocks than there are SMs, or into an exact multiple of their number.

Example: The A100 has 108 SMs. Suppose each SM holds one block at a time and every block takes the same time. Then 216 blocks finish in exactly two rounds, while 217 need three, the third with only 1 SM working, so the total time grows by half.
