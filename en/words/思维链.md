A series of intermediate reasoning steps that a model writes out before giving its answer. It was first elicited by prompting: writing out the solution process in examples, or adding “Let’s think step by step” after the question. Today's reasoning models are trained by reinforcement learning such as [[GRPO]] to write long chains of thought on their own before answering.

Note: Writing out intermediate steps helps because the computation per token is fixed. Answering in one step leaves the model a single forward pass of computation; spreading the reasoning over many tokens gives it more computation and intermediate results to refer to. The price is longer output, with higher latency and cost. The ability emerges with scale; in the original paper it became clear only above roughly a hundred billion parameters.

Example: On GSM8K math problems, the 540-billion-parameter PaLM answers 17.9% correctly when answering directly and 56.9% with 8 chain-of-thought examples. With only that one-line prompt added, zero-shot, InstructGPT's accuracy on MultiArith rose from 17.7% to 78.7%.
