Cutting matrices into small tiles and loading one tile at a time into [[memory hierarchy|shared memory]] for repeated use, in order to raise the [[arithmetic intensity]]. To compute $C = AB$, a thread block takes one tile of $C$, loads the corresponding tiles of $A$ and $B$ in turn, multiplies and accumulates them in shared memory, then moves on to the next pair. Each number loaded is used many times instead of being read once per use.

Note: The tile size is a trade-off: larger tiles mean more reuse and higher intensity, but shared memory and registers are limited, and oversized tiles leave fewer blocks resident and lower the [[occupancy]]. High-performance matrix multiplication tiles once more at the register level.

Example: To multiply two $n \times n$ matrices, if each element of $C$ reads its own row and column from GPU memory, $2n^3$ numbers are read in all. With $T \times T$ tiles, each number loaded is used $T$ times, and the reads fall to $2n^3 / T$. For $n = 4096$ and $T = 128$, they fall from about $1.4 \times 10^{11}$ numbers to about $1.1 \times 10^9$.
