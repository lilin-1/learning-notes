The empirical regularity by which a model's loss falls as a power law in its parameters, data and compute. Training a dense model with $N$ parameters on $D$ tokens takes about $C \approx 6ND$ floating-point operations. For a given $C$, the Chinchilla study found that parameters and data should grow in equal proportion. Chinchilla itself has 70 billion parameters trained on 1.4 trillion tokens, about 20 tokens per parameter.

Note: The regularity lets experiments on small models be extrapolated to the best configuration of a large one. Earlier models mostly had many parameters and little data. Gopher has 280 billion parameters; Chinchilla, a quarter of that size, was trained on 4 times the data with the same compute and performed better. Compute-optimal counts only training; the cost of inference grows with the parameters, so today's models are often smaller and trained on far more than 20 tokens per parameter.

Example: Llama 3 405B was trained on 15.6 trillion tokens, $6ND = 6 \times 4.05 \times 10^{11} \times 1.56 \times 10^{13} \approx 3.8 \times 10^{25}$ floating-point operations.
