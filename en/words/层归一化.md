An operation that standardizes the feature vector of a single example and then applies an elementwise affine map:

$$\mathrm{LayerNorm}(x) = \gamma \odot \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}} + \beta,$$

where $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are the mean and variance of the components of $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are learned parameters, and $\epsilon$ is a small positive number that prevents division by zero. For sequences, the vector at each position is normalized separately.

Note: Layer normalization is preferred to batch normalization because its statistics come from the example alone, not from the batch. Sequences differ in length and the batch size at inference may be 1, so batch statistics are unreliable; layer normalization is computed per position and behaves identically in training and inference.

Example: For $x = (2, 4, 6, 8)$, $\mu = 5$ and $\sigma^2 = 5$. With $\gamma = 1$, $\beta = 0$ and $\epsilon$ ignored, the output is about $(-1.34, -0.45, 0.45, 1.34)$.
