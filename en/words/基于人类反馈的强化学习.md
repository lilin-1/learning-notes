Fine-tuning a [[language model]] by reinforcement learning with the scores of a [[reward model]] as the reward, abbreviated RLHF. InstructGPT takes three steps: first [[instruction tuning]], then training a reward model on human comparisons, and finally raising the scores of the model's responses with [[PPO]]. The objective subtracts the [[KL divergence]] from the instruction-tuned model $\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}$, a KL penalty, to keep the model from drifting too far:

$$\max_\pi \ \mathbb{E}\big[r(x, y)\big] - \beta\, \mathbb{E}\left[\log \frac{\pi(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y \mid x)}\right].$$

The expectations average over prompts $x$ and the responses $y$ the model generates, $r$ is the reward model's score, and $\beta$ controls the strength of the penalty.

Note: The KL penalty limits how far the policy strays from its starting point: the reward model is reliable only near responses it has seen, and a policy that wanders too far learns to exploit it. The effect is striking: in InstructGPT's evaluation, people preferred the responses of a 1.3-billion-parameter model to those of the 175-billion-parameter GPT-3. The price is a complex pipeline that runs four models at once in training: the policy, the reference model, the reward model and the value model.

Example: A response has reward 2, and its log-probability under the current model exceeds that under the reference model by 5. With $\beta = 0.02$, it contributes $2 - 0.02 \times 5 = 1.9$ to the objective.

Paper: [Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) (Ouyang et al., 2022)
