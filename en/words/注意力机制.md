An operation that sums values, each weighted by how well a query matches the corresponding key. For a query $q$ and key–value pairs $(k_1, v_1), \dots, (k_m, v_m)$, the output is

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha_i v_i, \quad (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m) = \mathrm{softmax}\big(s(q, k_1), \dots, s(q, k_m)\big),$$

where $s$ is the scoring function. The weights are normalized by [[softmax]], so they are nonnegative and sum to $1$.

Note: The score $s(q, k_i)$ only decides the weighting and is not the same as the [[logits]] of a model's output layer. Both are normalized by softmax, but the distribution of scores ranges over the positions of a sequence, whereas the distribution of logits ranges over the tokens of the vocabulary.

Example: With the dot product as scoring function, let $q = (1, 0)$, $k_1 = (1, 0)$, $k_2 = (0, 1)$, $v_1 = 10$ and $v_2 = 20$. The scores are $(1, 0)$, the weights about $(0.73, 0.27)$, and the output $0.73 \times 10 + 0.27 \times 20 = 12.7$, leaning toward the value of $k_1$, which matches the query better.
