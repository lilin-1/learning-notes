A model that assigns probabilities to sequences of [[token|tokens]]. By the chain rule,

$$P(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \prod_{t=1}^{n} P(x_t \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}),$$

so it suffices to predict the distribution of the next token given the preceding ones; training uses [[cross-entropy]] as the loss.

Note: This distribution comes from the output layer. The last layer's vector at the current position, $h \in \mathbb{R}^d$, is multiplied by a $d \times |\mathcal{V}|$ matrix, giving [[logits]] for every token in the vocabulary. [[softmax|Softmax]] turns the logits into probabilities, and many models share this matrix with the [[embedding]] matrix. Finding the most probable token therefore needs no search: one matrix multiplication yields all the probabilities, and the largest is taken.

Example: If $P(\text{cat}) = 0.1$, $P(\text{sat} \mid \text{cat}) = 0.3$ and $P(\text{down} \mid \text{cat}, \text{sat}) = 0.4$, then $P(\text{cat}, \text{sat}, \text{down}) = 0.012$.
