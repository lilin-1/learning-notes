A way of training that runs the forward and backward computations in 16-bit floating point while keeping a 32-bit master copy of the weights, on which the updates are made. There are two 16-bit formats. FP16 has 5 exponent bits and 10 mantissa bits, with a maximum of about 65504; BF16 has 8 exponent bits and 7 mantissa bits, with the same range as 32-bit floating point. 16-bit matrix multiplications run on the [[Tensor Core|Tensor Cores]], much faster than in 32 bits, and the memory for activations and gradients is halved.

Note: The master weights stay in 32 bits because each update is often several orders of magnitude smaller than the weight, and adding it in 16 bits would round it away. With FP16, small gradients also underflow to 0, so the loss is first multiplied by a large number and the gradients divided by it afterwards, which is called loss scaling. BF16 has the same range as 32 bits, so small gradients rarely underflow; training today mostly uses it and generally no longer needs loss scaling.

Example: Adjacent BF16 numbers near 1 are $2^{-7} \approx 0.0078$ apart. With a weight of 1 and an update of $0.001$, $1 + 0.001$ rounds back to 1 in BF16 and the update is lost; 32-bit floating point has a spacing of $2^{-23}$ and keeps it.
