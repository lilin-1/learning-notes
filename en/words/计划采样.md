A method that gradually replaces the true context with the model's own predictions during training, to mitigate [[exposure bias]]. At training step $i$, a coin is flipped for each position: with probability $p_i$ the true previous token is used, otherwise the token the model predicted. $p_i$ decreases gradually from near 1, for example by inverse sigmoid decay:

$$p_i = \frac{k}{k + e^{i/k}}, \quad k \ge 1.$$

Note: At first the context is almost entirely true, so the model learns the correct conditions; later it sees more of its own predictions and learns to continue after its mistakes. Using its own predictions from the start makes training fail: on the original paper's image captioning task, BLEU-4 fell from 28.8 to 11.2, whereas the scheduled decay raised it to 30.6. There are two costs: its underlying training objective has been shown to be improper, and applied to a Transformer, the context must first be predicted by the model, which loses the parallelism of [[teacher forcing]].

Example: Take $k = 1000$. At $i = 0$, $p_0 = 1000 / 1001 \approx 0.999$; at $i = k \ln k \approx 6908$, $e^{i/k} = k$ and $p_i = 0.5$; at $i = 2k \ln k \approx 13816$, $p_i = 1 / 1001 \approx 0.001$.

Paper: [Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099) (Bengio et al., 2015)
