Training a small model (the student) on the probability distribution output by a large model (the teacher). The [[logits]] of both are divided by a temperature $T$ and passed through [[softmax]], and the loss is the [[cross-entropy]] between the student's and the teacher's distributions, often plus the cross-entropy with the true labels. The gradient of the soft-target term shrinks as $1/T^2$, so it is multiplied by $T^2$.

Note: The teacher's distribution carries more information than a one-hot label: besides the correct answer, it shows which other candidates are plausible and how plausible each is. Distillation of large models is also often simpler: the student is fine-tuned in a supervised way on text the teacher generated, learning the teacher's outputs rather than its distribution.

Example: DeepSeek used 800,000 samples generated by R1 for supervised fine-tuning alone, distilling its reasoning ability into small Qwen and Llama models. According to the first version of the paper, the distilled 32-billion-parameter model reached 72.6% on AIME 2024. A model of the same size trained with reinforcement learning alone reached only 47.0%.

Paper: [Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) (Hinton et al., 2015)
