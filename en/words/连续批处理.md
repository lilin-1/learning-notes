A scheduling method that forms batches per [[prefill and decode|decode]] step rather than per request, also called iteration-level scheduling. At the end of every step, sequences that have generated an end token leave at once and waiting requests join at once, so the GPU does not wait for the longest sequence in the batch to finish. In static batching, the slot of a sequence that finishes early stays empty until the whole batch completes.

Note: This works because the computations of the sequences in a batch are independent of one another. They share only the reading of the weights within one forward pass, so joining or leaving at any time does not affect the others' results. Combined with on-demand cache allocation, it lets batches be both large and full.

Example: A batch of 4 sequences has output lengths 10, 10, 10 and 100. Static batching runs 100 steps, 400 step-slots in all, of which only 130 do work, a utilization of about 33%. Continuous batching lets new requests fill the 3 empty slots right after step 10.

Paper: [Orca: A Distributed Serving System for Transformer-Based Generative Models](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yu) (Yu et al., 2022)
