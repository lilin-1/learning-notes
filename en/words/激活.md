The intermediate results computed by the layers of a [[neural network]], that is, the inputs and outputs of each layer, as distinct from the weights, which are parameters; the name comes from the outputs of [[activation function|activation functions]]. A token's activation at a layer is a $d$-dimensional vector, and each dimension is called a channel. The activations of $n$ tokens form an $n \times d$ matrix whose $j$-th column holds the values of channel $j$ across the tokens.

Note: Activations differ from weights in changing with the input. Weights are fixed after training and can be quantized or pruned in advance; activations differ in every forward pass and can only be handled during computation. In training, backpropagation needs the activations of the forward pass, which must be kept until the backward pass finishes, so their memory grows with the batch size and sequence length. In large models, a few channels of the activations are often far larger than the rest, and for almost every token; these are called outlier channels.

Example: Two tokens have activations $(0.2, 60, -0.1, 0.3)$ and $(-0.3, 55, 0.2, 0.1)$; channel 2 is an outlier channel. Quantized to 8-bit integers with one scale factor shared by all four channels, the factor is set by 60, at $60 / 127 \approx 0.47$. The other channels can only take 0 or $\pm 0.47$ and become $(0, 0, 0.47)$ and $(-0.47, 0, 0)$, losing almost all their information.
