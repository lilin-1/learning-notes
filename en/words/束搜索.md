A decoding method that keeps, at every step, only the $k$ partial sequences with the highest cumulative probability and extends each of them; $k$ is called the beam width. With $k = 1$ it is [[greedy decoding]]; a larger $k$ comes closer to exhaustive search at a higher cost. In practice, sequences are scored by the sum of their log probabilities, normalized by length so as not to favor short sequences.

Example: Let $P(a) = 0.5$ and $P(b) = 0.4$, and let the most probable tokens after them have probabilities $0.4$ and $0.9$. With $k = 2$, the first step keeps both $a$ and $b$; the second compares $0.5 \times 0.4 = 0.2$ with $0.4 \times 0.9 = 0.36$ and picks the sequence starting with $b$, whereas greedy decoding would pick $a$.
