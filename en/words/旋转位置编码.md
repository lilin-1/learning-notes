A [[positional encoding]] that acts on queries and keys by rotation, abbreviated RoPE. The $d$ components of a vector are grouped into $d/2$ pairs, each viewed as a point in the plane, and at position $p$ the $i$-th pair is rotated about the origin by $p\,\theta_i$ radians:

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos p\theta_i & -\sin p\theta_i \\ \sin p\theta_i & \cos p\theta_i \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}.$$

Pair $0$ rotates fastest, and later pairs ever more slowly. Rotation matrices satisfy $R_a^{\top} R_b = R_{b-a}$, so the dot product of a query at position $p$ with a key at position $p'$ depends only on $p - p'$. The rotation is applied in the [[self-attention]] of every layer, not to the input [[embedding]].

Note: Unlike positional encodings added to the input, the rotation acts only on queries and keys and leaves the values alone: position affects the attention weights without being mixed into the content that is gathered. Relative position appears directly in the dot product, so the model need not infer distances from absolute positions. Short-range differences are carried mainly by the fast pairs and long-range ones by the slow pairs, which together cover every scale.

Example: Let $d = 4$, with speeds $\theta_0 = 1$ and $\theta_1 = 0.01$. Placing $q = (1, 0, 1, 0)$ at position $2$ turns pair $0$ by $2$ radians and pair $1$ by $0.02$, giving $q' \approx (-0.42, 0.91, 1.00, 0.02)$. With the same $q$ and $k$ at positions $5$ and $3$, or at $3$ and $1$, the dot product of pair $0$ is $\cos 2 \approx -0.42$ either way: it depends only on the difference of $2$.

Paper: [RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864) (Su et al., 2021)
