The mismatch in conditions between training and generation for an autoregressive model trained with [[teacher forcing]]: in training, the context at every position comes from real data, but in generation it comes from the model's own outputs. Once the model writes a context rarely seen in training, its later predictions rest on conditions it has never seen. The name was introduced by Ranzato and colleagues.

Note: The worry is error accumulation. Imitation learning theory shows that when training only on demonstrated trajectories with a per-step error probability $\epsilon$, the number of errors in $T$ steps can reach $T^2\epsilon$ in the worst case; training on trajectories the learner itself produces grows only linearly in $T$. The remedies all let the model see its own outputs during training, for example by gradually replacing the true context with predictions, or by optimizing whole sequences with reinforcement learning. How large the effect is remains debated: one study found the distortion in open-ended generation limited, with the model able to recover by itself.

Example: Take $T = 100$ and $\epsilon = 0.01$. Training on demonstrated trajectories, the bound on errors is $100^2 \times 0.01 = 100$, meaning every step may go wrong; training on its own trajectories, the bound is of the order of $100 \times 0.01 = 1$.
