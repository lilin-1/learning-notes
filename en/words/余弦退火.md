A schedule that lowers the learning rate smoothly along a cosine curve from a maximum $\eta_{\max}$ to a minimum $\eta_{\min}$:

$$\eta_t = \eta_{\min} + \tfrac{1}{2}(\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min})\left(1 + \cos\frac{\pi t}{T}\right),$$

where $T$ is the total number of steps. Large models are commonly [[learning rate warmup|warmed up]] to $\eta_{\max}$ first and then annealed along the cosine to a tenth of it.

Note: The learning rate starts large and ends small: big steps early cross flat regions, small steps late converge near a minimum. The cosine changes slowly at both ends and falls fastest in the middle, which is smoother than stepwise decay at fixed moments and needs no choice of those moments.

Example: Let $\eta_{\max} = 3 \times 10^{-4}$ and $\eta_{\min} = 3 \times 10^{-5}$. At $t = T/2$, $\cos(\pi/2) = 0$ and the learning rate is the average of the two, $1.65 \times 10^{-4}$; at $t = T$ it reaches $3 \times 10^{-5}$.
