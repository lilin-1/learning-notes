Scaling the gradient down proportionally whenever its norm exceeds a threshold $c$, so that its norm becomes exactly $c$:

$$g \leftarrow g \cdot \min\!\left(1, \frac{c}{\|g\|}\right),$$

where $\|g\|$ is the 2-norm of the gradients of all parameters taken together. The direction is unchanged; only the length of a [[gradient descent]] step is limited.

Note: It guards against occasional large gradients. When a few samples are abnormal or training enters a steep region, the gradient can jump a hundredfold and push the parameters far away in a single step. Clipping acts only then; normally the norm is below the threshold and the gradient passes unchanged. Large models are commonly trained with $c = 1$.

Example: A gradient $(3, 4)$ has norm 5; with $c = 1$ it becomes $(0.6, 0.8)$. A gradient $(0.3, 0.4)$ has norm 0.5 and stays unchanged.

Paper: [On the difficulty of training Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5063) (Pascanu et al., 2012)
