An arithmetic unit in a [[streaming multiprocessor]] dedicated to small matrix multiply–accumulates. An ordinary core does one multiply–add at a time; a Tensor Core completes a small matrix multiplication in one instruction and accumulates it into the result. Its inputs are mostly 16-bit or lower-precision numbers, and accumulation is often in 32 bits. The matrix multiplications of large models run mainly on Tensor Cores.

Note: Tensor Cores raised the compute available for matrix multiplication by an order of magnitude, while bandwidth did not grow as fast, so the critical [[arithmetic intensity]] rose and more operations became memory-bound. Matrix dimensions should be multiples of 8, or efficiency drops.

Example: The ordinary cores of the A100 total 19.5 TFLOPS; its Tensor Cores reach 312 TFLOPS on 16-bit matrix multiplication, 16 times as much. The critical arithmetic intensity rises accordingly, from about 10 to about 156 operations per byte.
