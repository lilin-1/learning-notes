A model that summarizes an input sequence step by step in a hidden state of fixed size:

$$h_t = \bar{A} h_{t-1} + \bar{B} x_t, \quad y_t = C h_t.$$

It derives from continuous-time linear systems, with $\bar{A}$ and $\bar{B}$ obtained from continuous parameters by discretization. Mamba makes $\bar{B}$, $C$ and the discretization step depend on the input, which is called selectivity: the model can decide by content what to remember and what to forget.

Note: As with [[linear attention]], generation updates only a state of fixed size at each step, with no cache growing with the length, so batches can be larger and throughput higher. Training can still compute a whole sequence in parallel. The weakness is also shared: the state is finite, and exact retrieval of distant content is harder than with attention. Models today therefore often mix the two, with most layers using a state space model or linear attention and a few keeping attention.

Example: In Jamba, only 1 of every 8 layers is attention and the other 7 are Mamba. Qwen3-Next uses a ratio of 3 to 1: of its 48 layers, 36 are Gated DeltaNet, a form of linear attention, and 12 are attention.
