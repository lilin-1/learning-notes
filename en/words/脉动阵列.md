A circuit of multiply–add units arranged in a square grid, dedicated to matrix multiplication. Every clock cycle, data advances one step to neighboring units, moving in regular beats like blood pumped by the heart, hence the name. A common arrangement loads the weights into the units first, streams the inputs in from one side, and passes partial sums along the other direction as they accumulate. Each number read flows through a whole row of units and is used many times, so the [[arithmetic intensity]] is high without relying on large caches.

Note: Units connect only to their neighbors, so the wiring is short, control is simple, and nearly all the area goes to multiply–adds. Matrix engines on FPGAs often use it, and so do Google's TPUs. The price is a fixed array shape: when a matrix is smaller than the array, some units sit idle.

Example: The first-generation TPU has a $256 \times 256$ array of 65536 8-bit multiply–add units running at 700 MHz. Its peak is $65536 \times 2 \times 7 \times 10^8 \approx 9.2 \times 10^{13}$ operations per second, or 92 TOPS. Yet each cycle only 256 numbers need to be fed in from one side, each of them used 256 times.
