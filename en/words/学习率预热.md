The practice of raising the learning rate of [[gradient descent]] gradually from a very small value to its target early in training; after the warmup, the rate usually decays gradually. One explanation: early in training, [[Adam]] estimates the second moment from only a few steps of gradients, so the estimate has high variance and the step size can get out of control.

Example: The original [[Transformer]] uses the learning rate

$$\eta_s = d^{-0.5} \cdot \min\!\big(s^{-0.5},\ s \cdot w^{-1.5}\big)$$

at step $s$, with $w = 4000$. It rises linearly for the first $w$ steps and then decays as $s^{-0.5}$; with $d = 512$, the peak is about $7.0 \times 10^{-4}$.
