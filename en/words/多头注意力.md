Attention that computes [[scaled dot-product attention]] in parallel over $h$ sets of projections, then concatenates and projects the results. The $i$-th head is

$$\mathrm{head}_i = \mathrm{Attention}(QW_i^Q, KW_i^K, VW_i^V),$$

and the output is $\mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{head}_1, \dots, \mathrm{head}_h)\,W^O$, with every $W$ learned.

Note: The benefit of multiple heads lies not in more parameters or computation but in diverse weight distributions. With a single head, each position forms its weighted sum under only one distribution, and different relations are averaged together; multiple heads let the same position gather information under several distributions, each in its own subspace. Trained models often show a division of labor: some heads attend to the previous token, others to syntactically related words.

Example: The original design takes $d = 512$ and $h = 8$, so $d_k = d_v = d/h = 64$. The four projection matrices hold $4 \times 512^2 \approx 1.05$ million parameters, and the total computation is close to that of a single full-dimensional head.
