Training a model to generate whole sentences, with a sentence-level evaluation metric rather than the per-token [[cross-entropy]] as the objective. Metrics such as BLEU are not differentiable, so they are optimized through reinforcement learning or expected risk: the model samples whole sentences itself, and well-rated sentences become more probable. The model is thus trained on contexts it generated, and [[exposure bias]] is reduced.

Note: There are two representative approaches. MIXER trains with cross-entropy first, then, starting from the end of the sentence, switches more and more positions to a reinforcement learning objective. Minimum risk training samples about 100 candidates for each source sentence and minimizes the expectation of their losses, weighted by model probability. The price is much slower training because of sampling and evaluation, plus high-variance gradients, so both start from a model trained with cross-entropy. Another study found that minimum risk training reduces hallucinations in translations under domain shift.

Example: Two candidate translations have model probabilities 0.6 and 0.4 and BLEU 0.3 and 0.9; with loss $1 - \mathrm{BLEU}$, the expected risk is $0.6 \times 0.7 + 0.4 \times 0.1 = 0.46$. Training raises the probability of the second translation; with probabilities 0.2 and 0.8, the expected risk falls to $0.2 \times 0.7 + 0.8 \times 0.1 = 0.22$.

Paper: [Sequence Level Training with Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06732) (Ranzato et al., 2015)
Paper: [Minimum Risk Training for Neural Machine Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02433) (Shen et al., 2015)
