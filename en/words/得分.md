The unnormalized real numbers that a [[neural network]] feeds into [[softmax]]; in multiclass classification there is one per class. They may be positive or negative and need not sum to $1$; only after softmax do they form a probability distribution.

Note: Strictly, a model outputs logits; “outputs probabilities” is shorthand for “outputs logits that then pass through softmax”. Changes to the logits directly shape the distribution: adding a constant to all of them leaves the probabilities unchanged, while multiplying all of them by a number greater than $1$ sharpens the distribution. In training, the loss is often computed directly from the logits, which is numerically more stable.

Example: Logits $(2, 1, 0)$ give probabilities of about $(0.67, 0.24, 0.09)$. Adding $10$ to each changes nothing; doubling them to $(4, 2, 0)$ gives about $(0.87, 0.12, 0.02)$.
